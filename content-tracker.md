### Post 01 — 2026-09-01 | IT Resilience: Incidents, postmortems, and preventing repeat failures
- **Pillar:** Lesson / How I Work
- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Ashare%3A7500535888960163840
- **Hook:** *"Incidents, postmortems, and the changes that started them""*
- **Full Text:**
  > Incidents, postmortems, and the changes that started them"
  > " "
  > "The first five minutes of an incident call set the tone for everything that follows."
  > " "
  > "Early in my career, I'd open with "walk me through what happened." Diagnostic, but it puts the engineer who's already stressed into a position of justifying themselves before we've even scoped the problem."
  > " "
  > "Now it's closer to: "What do we know right now, and what do you need from me to keep digging?" Same information, different effect — the call stays focused on solving the problem instead of building a case."
  > " "
  > "That mindset has to carry into the postmortem, especially when the incident traces back to a change that went through formal approval."
  > " "
  > "If a Change Advisory Board signed off and it still broke, the postmortem has more to examine than "what went wrong in the code" — it has to look at whether the review process actually evaluated risk, tested successfully, or just processed a ticket."
  > " "
  > "Skipping that half of the question means the same gap in the process produces the same class of incident again."
  > " "
  > "The other trap: postmortems that get dismissed as "not worth a full review" because the incident was short, or low-severity, or fixed quickly."
  > " "
  > "Severity/Priority is the wrong filter. The right question is always: what can we solve and fix here to stop this specific failure mode from happening again? A 10-minute blip with an unclear root cause is worth more scrutiny than a two-hour outage with an obvious one."
  > " "
  > "And the highest-leverage fix rarely shows up in the postmortem at all — it's proactive."
  > " "
  > "Teams that invest in real logging and observability before things break spend far less time reacting to incidents in the first place. Reactive incident response has a visible cost — downtime, war rooms, postmortems."
  > " "
  > "Poor logging has an indirect cost that's just as real but harder to see: the hours spent guessing instead of knowing, on every incident, for as long as the gap goes unfixed."
  > " "
  > "Fewer surprises, not just fewer changes. That's the actual goal — and it starts well before the incident call.

  > "Can evaluate and manage the platforms and vendors the team depends on"
  > "Can build and coach a team that can do hands-on work, and know how to get out of their way"
