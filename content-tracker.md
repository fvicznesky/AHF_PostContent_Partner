### Post 01: 2026-11-20 — Concrete Networking Asks

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stanley-fred-vicznesky_since-march-ive-heard-ill-ask-around-share-7529569456877031424-jf3O/
- **Pillar/Format:** Lesson / How I Work
- **Hook:** *"Since March, I've heard 'I'll ask around' more times than I can count. I've stopped reading it as a brush-off. It's what people say when the ask in front of them is too big to act on."*
- **Key Message:** Turn vague asks into forwardable paragraphs with a specific role, geography, and industry (e.g., infrastructure & reliability leadership in Midwest banking/insurance).
- **Full Text:**
  > Since March, I've heard 'I'll ask around' more times than I can count. I've stopped reading it as a brush-off. It's what people say when the ask in front of them is too big to act on.
  >
  > That part is fixable, and it's on me, not them.
  >
  > An ask that gets acted on has a name, a company, and a role. 'Do you know anyone hiring?' gets a shrug. 'Do you know who runs infrastructure at a bank in Cincinnati, and would you introduce me?' gets an answer, because the person can picture the answer in five seconds.
  >
  > So I rewrite my asks now: 'Anyone in your network running platform or reliability at an insurer or bank in the Midwest? If someone comes to mind, I'd like an introduction, and I'll send them the note myself so you don't have to explain me.
  >
  > The last part matters more than it sounds. Hand someone a forwardable paragraph, not a document.
  >
  > If you've said 'let me ask around' this year, you're not the problem. You were handed an ask that was too heavy to carry.
